# Two Partitions That Must Be Backed Up Before Flashing

### 1. Introduction to the Backup Partitions

The EFS partition is used to store important information such as the phone's IMEI serial number, wireless network card, MAC address, and network settings. It has currently been found that upgrading a ROM with an unknown baseband, frequent flashing, or an incorrect double wipe can cause EFS information loss, the IMEI serial number being cleared or set to zero, unknown baseband, inability to use mobile networks, and other failures.

The PERSIST partition is an ext4 partition of the system. This partition contains DRM-related files, the sensor registry, and your Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, and MAC addresses are all here. It usually causes issues such as the compass not moving, the gyroscope not responding, the camera taking several seconds to open, abnormal screen-off during calls, and so on.

Having said all this, I just want to tell you that these two partitions are relatively important, because they are dynamic, and not every phone is exactly similar. If either one is damaged, even cable flashing cannot recover it. The Persist partition is included in the cable-flash package, but generally the cable-flash script does not include flashing this partition. However, you can extract Persist.img from the cable-flash package and flash it via third-party REC to repair it. The EFS partition, however, is not so easy to fix: you need to restore it by modifying your personal device information using a QCN backed up by someone else, but this is somewhat difficult and rather complicated.
