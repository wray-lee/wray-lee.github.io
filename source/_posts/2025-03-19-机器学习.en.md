## SVM

### Concepts

#### Two-Dimensional

-   Linearly separable: In binary classification, there exists a straight line that separates the two types of samples
-   Linearly inseparable: In binary classification, there does not exist a straight line that separates the two types of samples

##### Strict Definition

###### Ordinary Definition

A training sample set $\{(X ~ i ~,y ~ i ~),……,(X ~ N ~,Y ~ N ~)\}$, linearly separable for $i=1\sim N$ means there exist ($\omega_1$,$\omega_2$,$b$) such that for $i = 1 \sim N$:
$$
(1) if ~ y_i = +1, then ~ \omega_1 x_{i1} + \omega_2x_{i2} + b > 0	\\
(2) if ~ y_i = -1, then ~ \omega_1 x_{i1} + \omega_2x_{i2} + b < 0
$$

###### 向量定义

$$
X_i =
\begin{pmatrix}
x_{i1}	\\
x_{i2}
\end{pmatrix}^T

\qquad 

\omega=
\begin{pmatrix}
\omega_1\\
\omega_2
\end{pmatrix}^T

\\
(1)若 ~ y_i = +1, 则 ~ \omega^T X_i + b > 0	\\
(2) if ~ y_i = -1, then ~ \omega^T X_i + b < 0
$$

### 线性可分

#### 最优

1.   该直线分开了两类
2.   该直线最大化间隔（margin）
3.   该直线处于间隔的中间，到所有的支持向量距离相等

目的：

​	$最小化\dfrac{1}{2} \| \omega \| ^2 (\| \omega \| 为范数)$

#### 二次规划

1.   目标函数是二次项
2.   限制条件是一次项$y_i = \omega ^T x_i + b \geq 1, (i=1\sim N)$  [$\geq1$为归一化结果，可以通过参数缩放调整]

满足则为**凸优化问题**则无解或存在唯一最小极值

>   Choosing the functional margin equal to 1 is a normalization technique, the purpose of which is to make the problem have a unique solution. Without this normalization condition, $\omega$ and $b$ can be scaled by any ratio (for example, $2\omega, 2b$ would give the same hyperplane, but the functional margin would become twice as large), which would make the optimization objective unclear. By requiring $y_i (\omega^T x_i + b) \geq 1$, we fix the scale of the functional margin, thereby making the optimization problem meaningful.
>
>   Specifically:
>
>   - If we do not fix the functional margin at 1, but instead set it to some constant $k$, then $\omega$ and $b$ will be correspondingly scaled to $\frac{\omega}{k}$ and $\frac{b}{k}$, but the geometric margin $\frac{k}{\|\omega\|}$ remains unchanged.
>   - Therefore, choosing $k = 1$ is a convenient normalization method and will not affect the final geometric margin.



### Linearly Inseparable

Add slack variable (slack variable) **$\delta_i$**


$$
Minimize:	\\
\dfrac{1}{2} \| \omega \|^2 + C ~ \Sigma^N_{i=1} \delta_i ~ or ~ \dfrac{1}{2} \| \omega \|^2 + C ~ \Sigma^N _{i = 1} \delta_i^2	\\

Constraints:	\\
(1) ~ \delta_i \geq 0	\\
(2) ~ y_i = \omega^T x_i + b \geq 1 - \delta_i ~ , (i = 1 \sim N)	\\
$$

#### Kernel Function

$$
K(X_1,X_2) = \varphi(X_1)^T \varphi(X_2)
$$

Necessary and sufficient conditions

$$
1. ~ K(X_1,X_2) = K(X_2,X_1) \quad (commutativity)	\\
2. ~ \forall C_i (i = 1 \sim N), \forall N, \text{we have} \sum\nolimits^N_{i = 1} \sum\nolimits^N_{i = 1} C_i C_j K(X_i X_j) \quad (positive semidefiniteness)  	\\
$$


Exception:

**Gaussian kernel function:** $K(X_1 , X_2) = e^{-\frac{ \| x_1 - x_2 \| ^2}{2 \sigma ^2}}$



### Primal Problem and Dual Problem

-   **Primal problem:**
$$
Minimize: f(\omega)	\\
Constraints:	\\
g_i(\omega) \leq 0	\quad [i = 1\sim K]	\\
h_i(\omega) = 0	\quad [i = 1 \sim M]
$$

-   **Dual problem:**

$$
Definition (Lagrangian function): L(\omega, \alpha, \beta) = f(\omega) + \sum^K_{i = 1} \alpha_i g_i(\omega) + \sum^K_{i = 1} \beta_i h_i(\omega)	\\
=f(\omega + \alpha^T) g(\omega) +\beta h(\omega)	\\
\\
where ~ \alpha = [\alpha_1, \alpha_2, ..., \alpha_K]^T	\\
\beta = [\beta_1, \beta_2, ..., \beta_K]^T	\\
Inequality constraints (boundary): g(\omega) = [g_1(\omega), g_2(\omega), ..., g_K(\omega)]^T	\\
Equality constraints (normalization): h(\omega) = [h_1(\omega), h_2(\omega), ..., h_M(\omega)]^T	\\
$$

$$
Maximize: \theta(\alpha, \beta) = inf ~ L(\omega, \alpha, \beta), for all \omega in the domain	\\
Constraints: \alpha_i \geq 0, i = 1 \sim K
$$



**Theorem**:

Duality gap $f(\omega^*)-\theta(\alpha^*, \beta^*)$

1.   

$$
f(\omega^*) \geq \theta(\alpha^*, \beta^*) \\
\\

Proof:	\\
\theta(\alpha^*, \beta^*) = inf ~ L(\omega, \alpha^*, \beta^*)	\\
\leq L(\omega^*, \alpha^*, \beta^*)	\\
=f(\omega^*) + \alpha^{*T} g(\omega^*) + \beta^{*T}h(\omega^*)	\\
\leq f(\omega^*)
\\
\\
Given: ~ g(\omega^*) \leq 0; \quad h(\omega^*) = 0; \quad \alpha(\omega^*) \geq 0
$$



2.   When the objective function of the primal problem is convex and the constraints are linear functions,

$$
f(\omega^*) = \theta(\alpha^*, \beta^*)
$$

The duality gap is 0
