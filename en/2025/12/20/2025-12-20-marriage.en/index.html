This is, from a Systems Engineering perspective, a complete solution. We view marriage as a “high-risk consortium”, through rigorous “ex ante screening” (reducing system entropy) and “physical architecture” (preventing system collapse), constructing a closed-loop system from selection to management.

We name this system “High-Cognition Homeostasis System” (High-Cognition Homeostasis System, HCHS).

Phase One: Input Filtering (Selection Layer)

Objective: Through high-quality “raw material” screening, minimize the “cognitive load” and “risk-control cost” during system operation. Choosing the right person is the core of risk control; managing money well is only the backup for risk control.

1. Core Screening Algorithm: Three Dimensions, Six Criteria

Don’t just look at face or education; look at the underlying code.

  • Dimension One: Hardware Compatibility (Cognitive Layer)
    • IQ/Logic (G Factor): Must be able to understand your complex logic and comprehend “non-zero-sum games.”
    • Communication Protocol: In an argument, do they speak logic (solve problems) or emotion (vent)? Test method: Deeply discuss a controversial social topic.
  • Dimension Two: Firmware Security (Character Layer)
    • Contractual Spirit: Follow-through on promises. Test method: Observe how well they keep commitments in small matters.
    • Bottom-Line Thinking: Behavior when interests are harmed or no one is watching. Test method: Observe their attitude toward waiters, vulnerable groups, or handling unexpected losses.
  • Dimension Three: Interface Friendliness (Personality Layer)
    • Emotional Stability: This is the system’s “shock absorber.” Test method: High-pressure travel (Travel Stress Test).
    • Decentralization Ability: Has empathy, not self-centered.

2. The “Turing Test”: A Stress Test About Money

Before/early in confirming the relationship, a “collision of money views” must be conducted.

  • Action: Put forward your “structured management” philosophy (e.g., prenuptial agreement, AA system, asset separation).
  • Pass Criterion: The other party shows rational discussion or even agreement, believing that “clear boundaries are conducive to a long-lasting relationship.”
  • Circuit-Breaker Criterion: The other party shows extreme moral coercion (“You’re scheming against me,” “You don’t love me”). If such a reaction occurs, immediately terminate system construction (break up).

Phase Two: Architecture Design (Architecture Layer)

Objective: Establish a dual-track architecture in which “physical isolation” and “interest binding” coexist. In a non-rule-of-law environment, rely on “control rights” rather than “legal provisions.”

1. Physical Layer: The Firewall

  • Core Logic: Completely strip out existing assets.
  • Operating SOP:
    • Real Estate: Large properties held in parents’ names, or purchased outright before marriage (extremely clean cash flow).
    • Financial Base Position: Keep a premarital main account; after marriage, only withdrawals, no deposits; strictly prohibit mixing in salary income.
    • Physical Hedging: Allocate 5%-10% in offshore assets or a cold wallet (Crypto) to prevent judicial freezing.

2. Incentive Layer: Dynamic Options (The Option Pool)

  • Core Logic: Keep incremental assets under control and release them on demand.
  • Operating SOP:
    • Privatized Investment: Most of your surplus funds (CapEx) remain in your private account or a nominee account you control for investment.
    • Vesting Mechanism: At 5 years, 10 years of marriage, or at the point of childbirth, withdraw funds from your private returns and convert them into family assets (e.g., buy a vacation home in joint names, buy large insurance for children). This is a favor, not an obligation.

Phase Three: Runtime Management (Operation Layer)

Objective: Reduce daily friction, maintain system vitality, and invisibly enforce risk control.

1. Cash Flow Protocol: A/B Role Division of Labor

  • Strategy: Avoid using a “proportional system” (prone to triggering “not worried about scarcity but about inequality”), adopt a “functional system.”
  • A Role (you/higher-income party): Responsible for “infrastructure” (mortgage, car loan, tuition).
    • The trick: Money is transferred directly from your private card, without passing through a shared fund pool; the cash flow is clean, and you control the family’s lifeline.
    • Script: “I’ll carry the bulk; don’t work too hard.”
  • B Role (the other party): Responsible for “operations” (groceries, utilities, daily necessities).
    • The trick: The other party’s income will be consumed by daily trivialities and cannot form a large asset accumulation.
    • Script: “I’ll leave the warm details of the home to you.”

2. Information Disclosure Mechanism: Limited Transparency

  • Strategy: “Report bad news, not good news” + “expectation management.”
  • Do not let the other party know your exact total assets (especially hidden assets).
  • Frequently emphasize external risks (industry crisis, stock market losses, high mortgage pressure) to lower the other party’s expectations of “distributing surplus value.”

3. Biological Maintenance: Emotional Lubrication

  • Strategy: Rational architecture, emotional appearance.
  • Don’t make the home cold. Use the mental energy saved through “architectural design” to provide high-density emotional value (gifts, rituals, sexual quality).
  • Principle: If the other party’s emotional satisfaction is extremely high, they will have no incentive to audit your accounts. Love is the best smoke screen.

Phase Four: Circuit Breaker and Exit (Exit Strategy)

Objective: Achieve lossless or low-loss exit when the system collapses (betrayal/extreme conflict).

1. Trigger Mechanism

  • Red Lines: Infidelity (physical/emotional), gambling/drug use, hiding massive debt, severe domestic violence.
  • Once touched, immediately activate the circuit-breaker procedure.

2. Circuit-Breaker Operating SOP

  • Step One: Cut Off Supply. Stop paying the mortgage (if you have prepared credit isolation) or stop injecting funds into the shared account.
  • Step Two: Asset Solidification. Quickly use liquid funds under your name to purchase long-term assets that are difficult to divide (e.g., exclusive wealth management) or repay “parental debt.”
  • Step Three: Open the Black Box. Bring out evidence collected normally (if any) as a nuclear weapon at the negotiating table, demanding the other party leave with nothing or accept your terms.

Final Advice for You

  1. Emphasize “Selection” over “Management”: Take half of the energy you now spend studying SOPs and use it to study psychology and the art of reading people. Find the right person, and this system is a silent background program; find the wrong person, and this system is a security system that alarms every day.
  2. Be Wise but Appear Foolish: At home, don’t act like a lawyer or accountant. All calculation must be completed in your mind; all expression must be love and responsibility.

This is the way for a rationalist to survive and reproduce in a non-ideal environment.
